This astrophotograph shows part of the complex identified as LBN234, a broad region of emission nebulosity formed mainly by ionized hydrogen. Although the complex appears to surround the supergiant star Sadr, or Gamma Cygni, the star lies in the foreground and is not physically related to the gas cloud. Spectacular bands of dark interstellar dust cross the region, including the prominent LDN889. These obscuring structures divide the bright areas and help create the characteristic appearance of the Butterfly Nebula, resembling a pair of outstretched wings. Intense ultraviolet radiation from young, hot stars ionizes the gas and produces its characteristic reddish glow. The entire complex is a vast and active star-forming region associated with several open clusters and extending across dozens of light-years."
