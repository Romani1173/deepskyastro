The astrophotograph shows part of this complex identified as LBN 234, a broad region of emission nebulosity formed mainly by ionised hydrogen. Although the complex appears to surround the supergiant star Sadr, or Gamma Cygni, the star lies in the foreground and is not physically related to the gas cloud.

Spectacular bands of dark interstellar dust cross the region, including the prominent LDN 889. These obscuring structures divide the bright areas and help create the characteristic appearance of the Butterfly Nebula, resembling a pair of outstretched wings.

Intense ultraviolet radiation emitted by young, hot stars ionises the gas and produces its characteristic reddish glow. The entire complex is a vast and active star-forming region associated with several open clusters and extending across dozens of light-years.
